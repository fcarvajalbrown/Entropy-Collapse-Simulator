Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the research article "A Calibration-Free Entropy Index for Screening Progressive-Collapse Vulnerability of Steel Building Frames: Column Triage Validated Against Alternate-Load-Path Analysis" for consideration in Revista de la Construccion.

Progressive-collapse assessment by the alternate load path (ALP) method (GSA 2003; UFC 4-023-03) requires engineers to choose which columns to notionally remove and to run a costly nonlinear analysis for each, judged against structure-specific acceptance limits. This paper addresses that decision with a practical screening tool. The Entropy Robustness Index R_S ranks a steel frame's columns by the criticality of their loss from a single linear analysis, with no calibrated threshold. On the published Vogel (1985) six-storey two-bay steel moment frame, the entropy column ranking agrees with a code-style ALP severity (post-removal demand-capacity ratio) at a Spearman correlation of 0.91 across all eighteen columns, and the two columns whose loss most overstresses the frame are exactly the two the index flags first. The index also distinguishes design variants by their redundancy and identifies the members whose loss a weaker design cannot survive.

The contribution is a construction-engineering workflow: cheap linear triage that tells the engineer where the expensive nonlinear ALP effort is worth spending. The scope is presented honestly as that of a screening tool (planar, first-order, linear-elastic; a measure of load-path redundancy rather than strength, with no early-warning claim), and every number and figure is reproducible from openly available software.

I believe the topic fits the journal's scope in structures and construction and will be of interest to practitioners and researchers concerned with structural robustness and progressive collapse. Thank you for your consideration.
